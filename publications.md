## 2008

### Transition from squeezing to dripping in a microfluidic T-shaped junction
**Authors:** M. De Menech, P. Garstecki, F. Jousse, H. A. Stone
**Journal:** Journal of Fluid Mechanics, vol. 595, pp. 141–161, 2008
**DOI:** [10.1017/S002211200700910X](https://doi.org/10.1017/S002211200700910X)
**Citations:** 656
**Links:** [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/685ca9d6e0b8e2396d817b76f85d81811d8f39de) · [PDF](http://www.princeton.edu/~stonelab/Publications/pdfs/From%20Howard/JFM/DeMenechGarsteckiJousseStoneJFM08.pdf)

> We describe the results of a numerical investigation of the dynamics of breakup of streams of immiscible fluids in the confined geometry of a microfluidic T-junction. We identify three distinct regimes of formation of droplets: squeezing, dripping and jetting, providing a unifying picture of emulsification processes typical for microfluidic systems. The squeezing mechanism of breakup is particular to microfluidic systems, since the physical confinement of the fluids has pronounced effects on the interfacial dynamics.

## 2007

### Recovering hidden electronic states using energy-resolved imaging of metal clusters at surfaces
**Authors:** M. De Menech, U. Saalmann, M. E. Garcia
**Journal:** New Journal of Physics, vol. 9, 340, 2007
**DOI:** [10.1088/1367-2630/9/9/340](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/9/9/340)
**Citations:** 4
**Links:** [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/906f89da3886eee3fca7b440a036880957d68e8b)

> Low-temperature scanning tunneling spectroscopy (STS) allows us to probe electronic properties of clusters at surfaces with unprecedented accuracy. Recent experimental determination of the differential conductance of supported clusters yield considerable deviations with respect to the expected density of states and suggest that many cluster states are invisible to STS measurements. By means of fully self-consistent quantum transport calculations, using realistic tunneling tips, we show that, depending on the tip shape, only a small fraction of the electronic states contribute to the STS spectra, thus explaining the experimental findings. We demonstrate that the unambiguous characterization of the states on the supported clusters can be achieved with energy-resolved images.

## 2006

### Modeling of droplet breakup in a microfluidic T-shaped junction with a phase-field model
**Authors:** M. De Menech
**Journal:** Physical Review E, vol. 73, 031505, 2006
**DOI:** [10.1103/PhysRevE.73.031505](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.73.031505)
**Citations:** 104
**Links:** [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b96035c1a7ff12be40c0fdfa47b2ccadd11fa630) · [PDF (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0511371)

> A phase-field method is applied to the modeling of flow and breakup of droplets in a T-shaped junction in the hydrodynamic regime where capillary and viscous stresses dominate over inertial forces, which is characteristic of microfluidic devices. The transport equations are solved numerically in the three-dimensional geometry, and the dependence of the droplet breakup on the flow rates, surface tension and viscosities of the two components is investigated in detail. The model reproduces quite accurately the phase diagram observed in experiments performed with immiscible fluids. The critical capillary number for droplet breakup depends on the viscosity contrast, with a trend which is analogous to that observed for individual isolated droplets in hyperbolic flow.

### Energy-resolved STM mapping of C₆₀ on metal surfaces: a theoretical study
**Authors:** M. De Menech, U. Saalmann, M. E. Garcia
**Journal:** Physical Review B, vol. 73, 155407, 2006
**DOI:** [10.1103/PhysRevB.73.155407](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.73.155407)
**Citations:** 23
**Links:** [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/306f23b3e911dfb13e32b3ff24ac3e1ed8d52a4d) · [PDF (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0511601)

> We present a detailed theoretical study of scanning tunneling imaging and spectroscopy of C₆₀ on silver and gold surfaces. The surface/sample/tip system is described within a self-consistent DFT based tight-binding model. The topographic and conductance images are computed at constant current from a full self-consistent transport theory based on nonequilibrium Green's functions and compared with those simulated from the local density of states. The molecular orbitals of C₆₀ are clearly identified in the energy resolved maps, in close correspondence with the experimental results. We show how the tip structure and orientation can affect the images.

## 2002

### Turbulent self-organized criticality
**Authors:** M. De Menech, A. L. Stella
**Journal:** Physica A: Statistical Mechanics and its Applications, 2002
**DOI:** [10.1016/S0378-4371(02)00745-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-4371(02)00745-8)
**Citations:** 27
**Links:** [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b61a1881ee8a66f1e3c4403ef9de18fc02ebe150) · [PDF (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0103601)

> In the prototype sandpile model of self-organized criticality time series obtained by decomposing avalanches into waves, distinguished by the number of times the avalanche seed site has toppled, show intermittent fluctuations. The qth moments of wave size differences possess local multiscaling and global simple scaling regimes analogous to those holding for velocity structure functions in fluid turbulence. The correspondence involves identity of a basic scaling relation and of the form of relevant probability distributions. The sandpile provides a qualitative analog of many features of turbulent phenomena.

## 2000

### From waves to avalanches: two different mechanisms of sandpile dynamics
**Authors:** M. De Menech, A. L. Stella
**Journal:** Physical Review E, vol. 62, R4528, 2000
**DOI:** [10.1103/PhysRevE.62.R4528](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.62.R4528)
**Citations:** 31
**Links:** [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/391f52b920d7920a62cc52e8ccc2ae17bfe24709) · [PDF (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0002310)

> Time series resulting from wave decomposition show the existence of different correlation patterns for avalanche dynamics. For the d=2 Bak-Tang-Wiesenfeld model, long range correlations determine a modification of the wave size distribution under coarse graining in time, and multifractal scaling for avalanches. In the Manna model, the distribution of avalanche coincides with that of waves, which are uncorrelated and obey finite size scaling, a result expected also for the d=3 Bak-Tang-Wiesenfeld sandpile.

## 1999

### Multifractal Scaling in the Bak-Tang-Wiesenfeld Sandpile and Edge Events
**Authors:** C. Tebaldi, M. De Menech, A. L. Stella
**Journal:** Physical Review Letters, vol. 83, 3952, 1999
**DOI:** [10.1103/PhysRevLett.83.3952](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.83.3952)
**Citations:** 116
**Links:** [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df995bf62e0dda6cebc6b08a5e82802f79d4d184) · [PDF (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/9903270)

> A widely applicable analysis of numerical data shows that, while the distribution of avalanche areas obeys finite size scaling, that of toppling numbers is universally characterized by a full, nonlinear multifractal spectrum. Boundary effects determine an unusual dependence on system size of the moment scaling exponents of the conditional toppling distribution at a given area. This distribution is also multifractal in the bulk regime. The resulting picture brings to light unsuspected physics of this long-studied prototype model.

## 1998

### Rare events and breakdown of simple scaling in the abelian sandpile model
**Authors:** M. De Menech, A. L. Stella, C. Tebaldi
**Journal:** Physical Review E, vol. 58, R2677, 1998
**DOI:** [10.1103/PhysRevE.58.R2677](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.58.R2677)
**Citations:** 70
**Links:** [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5690aa8235c6ca1203aca6b711a6c8cf47dbafe0) · [PDF (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/9805045)

> Due to intermittency and conservation, the Abelian sandpile in 2D obeys multifractal, rather than finite size scaling. In the thermodynamic limit, a vanishingly small fraction of large avalanches dominates the statistics and a constant gap scaling is recovered in higher moments of the toppling distribution. Thus, rare events shape most of the scaling pattern and preserve a meaning for effective exponents, which can be determined on the basis of numerical and exact results.
